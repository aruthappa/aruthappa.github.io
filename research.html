Research philosophy

Where disciplines meet, solutions emerge.

Agroforestry sits at the intersection of production, ecology and people. My research is therefore designed to connect measurable ecosystem processes with technologies and management approaches that can work under real field conditions.

Ecology × ProductionDesigning tree–crop–livestock systems that strengthen productivity while improving ecological function.
Climate × CarbonExploring biomass, soil carbon, biochar and plantation-based pathways for climate mitigation and resilient landscapes.
Science × SocietyTranslating research into technologies, farmer guidance, policy insights and public-facing science communication.

Seven connected areas of inquiry

These domains represent the core scientific questions running across my research programmes, publications and field interventions.

01

Agroforestry Systems

Developing and evaluating tree-based production systems for improved resource use, productivity and resilience.

  • Tree–crop interactions
  • Cashew-based agroforestry
  • Silvopastoral and fodder systems
02

Carbon & Climate Solutions

Investigating carbon sequestration and practical climate-mitigation pathways within plantation and agroforestry landscapes.

  • Biomass and carbon assessment
  • Biochar-based interventions
  • Carbon-credit potential
03

Soil Health & Land Restoration

Understanding soil processes and developing nature-based approaches for degraded, saline and stressed landscapes.

  • Salinity-tolerance approaches
  • Land rehabilitation
04

Bamboo-Based Systems

Connecting bamboo ecology, agroforestry, restoration and value addition with emerging livelihood and market opportunities.

Coastal & Saline Agroforestry

Using remote sensing, GIS and machine learning to map, monitor and understand agroforestry and landscape processes.

02 · The research question

How can tree-based systems make landscapes more productive, resilient and ecologically functional?

This question connects my work across agroforestry, carbon, soil health, biodiversity and geospatial science. The emphasis is not only on measuring ecosystem services, but on identifying management pathways that can be translated into field practice.

03 · From research to application

A five-stage pathway

01 · IdentifyDefine a field-relevant ecological or production problem.
02 · MeasureGenerate evidence through experiments, observations and quantitative analysis.
03 · IntegrateCombine ecology, agronomy, soil science, climate and spatial technologies.
04 · TranslateConvert findings into technologies, guidance, demonstrations and policy insights.
05 · ImpactEvaluate adoption, ecosystem benefits and relevance beyond the experimental plot.
